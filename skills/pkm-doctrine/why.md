# Why the rules are what they are

This doctrine distills established personal-knowledge-management methods and
observed page-authoring corrections into a small, surface-configurable method.
The broad findings converge:

1. **The mind is for having ideas, not holding them.** Pages are externalized
   working memory; their job is to free attention.
2. **Trust requires completeness, currentness, and review.** Stale-looking
   pages damage confidence, but freshness claims must come from observed owner
   engagement—not an assistant rewrite timestamp.
3. **Execution and knowledge have edges.** An install has one authoritative
   task surface. PKM pages must not become a second inbox or duplicate task
   manager.
4. **Organize by actionability.** Projects end; areas are standards without
   finish lines; people and topics are not areas.
5. **Plan detail near the action.** Far-future task lists go stale. When the
   work approaches, derive the next useful detail from current evidence.
6. **One next thing.** A project often has one binding constraint. Multiple
   competing "Next" statements hide it.
7. **Sparse truth beats symmetrical filler.** An honest blank with one good
   question is better than invented completeness.
8. **The owner keeps meaning-making.** The assistant proposes, elicits, and
   maintains authorized surfaces; it does not invent taste or commitments.
9. **Stability is load-bearing.** Familiar structure supports spatial memory.
   Update content without unnecessary structural churn.
10. **A view sets state; it is not a dump.** Minimum relevant context and calm
    language beat exhaustive display.

The shipped pack is the general method. Each install supplies its writable PKM
surface, authoritative task surface, restriction adapter, registry, and
personal overlay.
