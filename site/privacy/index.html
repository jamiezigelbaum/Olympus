Privacy policy

Effective 14 October 2026.

Olympus is a knowledge engine that runs on your computer (a Mac, or a Linux computer running Omarchy) and answers questions about your own mail, files and notes, including questions you ask in ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or another AI app you connect. It is published by Open Coordination Unlimited, Inc. ("OCU", "we", "us"), a United States company, which is responsible for the personal data described here. This policy explains what data Olympus processes, where, why, who receives it, how long it is kept, and how you control it. When it changes, the date above changes.

In short: Olympus stores your source collection and the index it builds on your computer; we do not maintain a hosted copy. When you ask a question in ChatGPT, ChatGPT receives the question it sent, plus answers and excerpts built from items Olympus has labeled Personal, with citations. Items labeled Private are excluded from what ChatGPT's model receives; answers from them are shown to you in an Olympus panel inside ChatGPT, encrypted between that panel and your computer. Requests between ChatGPT and your computer pass through our relay, which forwards them without intentionally storing their content. Claude, Grok and other AI apps you connect receive the same kinds of results from Personal items; for Private items they receive only answers written from them on your computer (by Olympus's private model, or by Venice if you set it up), never the items themselves, though those answers can repeat what the items say. When you connect Google, your computer signs in with Google directly. If you set up anonymous questions, a question you choose to ask goes to zkAPI and an AI model provider without your identity attached. Automatic labels can be wrong.

OCU holds little personal data, because Olympus runs on your computer. Olympus does not require an OCU user account, and OCU does not currently take payments for Olympus. What we hold:

  • Relay registry: for each install, an install identifier derived from the install's public key, the public key, and timestamps.
  • Relay event logs: event types (for example "session ready"), times, fixed diagnostic fields and a shortened one-way hash of the install identifier.
  • In memory only: IP addresses and request counters used to limit abuse, and which installs are online.
  • In transit: your questions, answers, evidence excerpts and the other data described in What ChatGPT receives, while the relay forwards them between ChatGPT, Claude or Grok and your computer.
  • Google sign-in: when you connect Gmail or Google Drive through Olympus's shared Google app (the default), your computer signs in with Google and refreshes its tokens with Google directly; no Olympus service takes part. On your computer, under your control and not collected by us, Olympus processes the content of the sources you connect, the index it builds, sign-in tokens for your accounts, your settings and local logs (see Categories of data).

The three labels

Olympus labels every item, one by one, as Personal, Private or Secret. Item names and titles are labeled separately from their contents, and are Personal by default, so the name of a Private item may be visible in ChatGPT even though its contents are not.

  • Personal items may be used to answer questions in ChatGPT and the other AI apps you connect.
  • Private items (for example health, money, legal and identity matters, and whatever you tell Olympus is private for you) are excluded from the answers and excerpts ChatGPT's model receives. They are answered on your computer, by the private model built into Olympus or by Venice if you choose to set it up, and the answer is shown to you in the private answer panel (see The private answer panel). Other AI apps you connect receive only answers written from them on your computer, never their text (see What other AI apps receive).
  • Secret items (for example passwords, recovery codes and private keys) that Olympus recognizes are never sent anywhere once recognized, and their contents are kept out of Olympus's search index; they are excluded from answer retrieval and model inputs. In the index, Olympus keeps where each one is: the source, its location, a screened title, the kinds of secret found and a one-way hash of the content. If an item becomes Secret after Olympus has indexed it, including when you mark it Secret, its contents are removed from the index, where Olympus keeps only that record of where it is. Telegram and WhatsApp messages stay in the raw message store on your computer until you remove that source, except that a message Olympus recognizes as Secret has its text (and any saved voice note) removed from that store. If you turn on Venice for reading files, Olympus first reads each image or PDF page on your computer and does not send it to Venice if it recognizes a secret in it or cannot read it; a secret that this local reading misses can still reach Venice. Recognition looks for common forms, such as private keys, API keys and labeled passwords, and can miss others; an item it misses is labeled like any other item.

Labeling is automatic and can be wrong. A new item with nothing that marks it as your own record is labeled Personal at once; Olympus then checks it on your computer and moves it to Private if the check finds it is, and an item whose check cannot finish is treated as Private. If an AI app read an item while it was Personal, what that app received stays with it, and an answer already being prepared when the item moves to Private can still be delivered. A copy made on your computer during that time stays on your computer and is never sent anywhere.

Categories of data

Processed on your computer

  • Content from sources you connect: mail, documents and files from accounts you connect, such as Gmail, Google Drive and Dropbox, and from other sources you set up on your computer, such as X bookmarks, Readwise, Telegram or WhatsApp. This can include any information those items contain, including text, images and audio.
  • Derived data: the search index, text extracted from documents and recordings, vector representations made by the built-in models, labels and their reasons.
  • Credentials: the sign-in tokens that let Olympus read the accounts you connect, the keys and tokens that link your computer to ChatGPT, and, if you set up anonymous questions, your zkAPI account keys.
  • Settings and logs: your Olympus settings and local log files. Olympus's logs are designed not to contain message content or tokens.

What ChatGPT (OpenAI) receives

Olympus returns data to ChatGPT in two ways: tool results that ChatGPT's model reads, and Olympus panels displayed inside ChatGPT. Panel-only data is not given to ChatGPT's model, but it passes through OpenAI's systems.

  • Questions and answers: the questions ChatGPT sends to Olympus on your behalf; answers and evidence excerpts built from Personal items; citations with source name, title, date and link where available; item names that are Personal, which can include the name of an item whose contents are Private; how many items were searched or not yet read; and temporary identifiers used to collect an answer that is still being prepared.
  • When Private items match: a fixed note to ChatGPT's model saying so, with no titles or content. For the private answer panel only: how many Private items matched, progress, a temporary job identifier and a public encryption key.
  • The Olympus dashboard and status: connection and source status, when sources last synced and what was found, indexing and download progress, item counts, names of files Olympus could not read and why, model choices and their status, recent activity times, troubleshooting status and diagnostic references. When you set up remote access, it also shows the configured SSH user name or host name and local service port, to give connection instructions.
  • Choosing folders and mail (panel only): folder names, identifiers or paths, how folders are nested, sizes and file counts; mail label names and identifiers, mail categories and counts; suggested sender addresses with how many messages each sent; the sender addresses you mark as always Private or skip; your saved choices; and technical revision markers that keep these choices consistent. Locations you keep Secret are left out.
  • Connecting a source: the service you chose, a one-time sign-in link and when it expires.
  • Your privacy settings: your own description of what is private for you, and how many folders, labels or senders you marked as always Private; the panel also shows which ones.
  • Cited sources: when you open a cited source that is not on your computer, its web address, which ChatGPT opens for you.
  • Anonymous questions asked in the conversation: the answer, the model that gave it, the exact question that was sent, the privacy level and preparation you chose, and whether network privacy was verified (see Anonymous questions).
  • The private question panel: ChatGPT's model learns only that the panel opened, or why it could not. The panel receives a temporary job identifier, a public encryption key and your saved level; the question you type and its answer are encrypted between the panel and your computer.
  • Errors: fixed error messages and, for some errors, an opaque diagnostic reference.

The private answer panel

When Private items match a question, an Olympus panel inside ChatGPT shows you an answer from them, marked "Not sent to ChatGPT". Your computer writes the answer and encrypts it with a key that only that panel holds; the panel fetches it from your computer through the relay, not through ChatGPT's conversation. ChatGPT's model does not receive it, and the relay forwards only encrypted data. The panel's code is served inside ChatGPT, so this protection also depends on your computer, the panel's code as ChatGPT delivers it, and the key exchange.

The panel keeps its encryption keys, job identifiers, creation times and question-panel progress in the browser storage ChatGPT gives it. Entries older than 24 hours are rejected or removed when the panel next uses or cleans that storage; unused entries can remain until a later cleanup or until you clear ChatGPT's site data. Answer text is not written to that storage.

What OpenAI does with your ChatGPT conversations, including Olympus results and panels, is governed by OpenAI's terms and privacy policy and your ChatGPT settings, not by this policy.

What other AI apps receive

Claude, Grok, Grok Bot and other AI apps you connect through the relay receive the questions they send to Olympus on your behalf, and answers, evidence excerpts and citations built from Personal items, like ChatGPT's model. They do not receive the text of items labeled Private or Secret. Olympus panels, including the private answer panel, are shown only in ChatGPT. When Private items match a question, the app receives an answer written from them by the private model on your computer, or by Venice if you set it up, checked for secrets, with each item's title, location, source and author, but never the items' text. If no such answer can be given, the question waits in Olympus on your computer, on its Private answers page, and the app may be told one fixed sentence that Private items may answer it.

Apps that run on your computer, such as Claude Code, Codex or Hermes, connect to Olympus there, without the relay, and receive the same. What any AI app's provider does with your conversations, including Olympus results, is governed by its own terms and privacy policy and your settings with it, not by this policy.

Anonymous questions (optional)

Anonymous questions are available on a Mac and on supported Linux x64 computers, such as Omarchy, once Olympus's network isolation checks pass. If you set it up on your computer, Olympus can ask a frontier AI model a question for you through zkAPI, using your own zkAPI account. It does this only when you ask for an anonymous or private question.

  • What leaves your computer: the prepared question and the technical information needed to route and run it, such as the chosen model. Olympus does not attach source documents or conversation history. In Strict mode, your own model on your computer first rewrites the question into general questions to remove identifying details; in Standard mode, by default, your model removes names, contact details and account numbers, or, if you choose, it goes as you wrote it or is prepared by your own instructions, which then decide what is removed; either way it can keep identifying details. Olympus checks it before it leaves.
  • Who receives it: the zkAPI service and the AI model provider it reaches, which can read the question. zkAPI is designed to separate who pays from what is asked, so your questions are not attached to your account. Olympus sends the question through Tor unless you turn Tor off, but cannot currently verify network anonymity on macOS. The content of a question can itself identify you.
  • Where you ask it: a question you write in the ChatGPT conversation, and its answer, are seen by OpenAI and pass through our relay readable, like other Olympus results. A question you type in the private question panel, and its answer, are encrypted between the panel and your computer.
  • What stays on your computer: your zkAPI account keys, and a usage record (counts, costs and technical checks for each session; not your questions or answers).
  • Your zkAPI account is your own, and is set up and managed in Olympus on your computer, outside ChatGPT. OCU does not sell zkAPI access or receive, hold or have custody of your zkAPI funds. Adding to the account is an Ethereum transaction, which is public on that network; zkAPI is designed so that your questions cannot be linked to it.

zkAPI and the model provider keep what they receive according to their own terms. OCU does not receive anonymous questions you type in the private question panel, or their answers, in readable form.

What the relay processes

ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Grok Bot reach your computer through the relay at mcp.olympusplugin.ai. Your computer keeps an encrypted, outgoing connection open to the relay, and the relay forwards their requests along it.

  • In transit: the relay ends the encrypted connection from the AI app (TLS), so request and response contents pass through its memory while being forwarded. Its code does not intentionally store them.
  • Logs: the relay's operational logs record event types, times, fixed diagnostic fields and a shortened one-way hash of your install's identifier. Its code does not intentionally log request or response content, tokens or credentials, query strings, or caller IP addresses. The web server in front of the relay is configured with access logging off.
  • Registry: for each install, an install identifier derived from its public key, the public key, and timestamps. It holds no name, email, IP address, account or token.
  • In memory only: IP addresses and counters used to limit abuse, and which installs are online.
  • Tokens: the relay does not issue or check the tokens these apps use. Your computer does.
  • Private answers and private questions: the relay forwards the panel's requests and your computer's replies encrypted, without the key to read them, and does not log them or their identifiers.

Our website

Our website, olympusplugin.ai, uses no cookies, analytics or trackers, loads nothing from other sites, and is configured with access logging off.

Olympus on your computer also checks olympusplugin.ai about once a day for a new version, by downloading the public install script. That check sends no identifiers, cookies or query string; our server sees your computer's IP address, as with any request to the website. Set OLYMPUS_UPDATE_CHECK=off to turn the check off.

Retention

  • On your computer: until you delete it. Uninstalling Olympus stops it but keeps its data until you delete that too (see Your controls).
  • Answers in progress: a finished pending answer stays available on your computer for about 15 minutes, so the AI app that asked can collect it.
  • Private panels: private answer jobs expire 10 minutes after they are created and private question jobs after 30 minutes. They are held in memory on your computer and removed by periodic cleanup. An answer already shown can stay visible while its panel is open. For the panel's browser storage, see The private answer panel.
  • Relay registry: active registrations expire after about 90 days without a connection. Earlier registry entries stay on disk until the relay restarts and compacts its records. The relay keeps up to 20,000 expired install identifiers, with no fixed time limit, to recognise returning installs, and keeps the identifiers of revoked installs until they are restored, so that a blocked install cannot reconnect.
  • Relay event logs: kept for 14 days, then deleted.
  • Sign-in return page: Cloudflare's request logs for the page that returns you after Dropbox sign-in, including the one-time code they may record, are kept under Cloudflare's own retention for our account; we use them only to troubleshoot. The Google sign-in service used by Olympus versions before 1.0.0 was retired before 1.0.0 was released, and the keyed account hashes and first-sign-in times it kept were deleted.
  • Support email: kept as long as needed to resolve your request, then deleted unless the law requires us to keep it longer.
  • Anonymous questions: Olympus keeps a usage record on your computer (counts, costs and session checks, not questions or answers) until you delete Olympus's data.
  • Account and billing data: Olympus does not require an OCU account and OCU does not currently take payments for Olympus, so we hold none.
  • OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, zkAPI, Venice and the providers you connect keep data according to their own policies.

Your controls

  • Choose sources: connect and disconnect sources in the Olympus dashboard.
  • Disconnect an AI app: remove Olympus in ChatGPT, Claude or Grok, or revoke the app's access on your computer from the Olympus dashboard or with olympus connections list and olympus connections revoke <id>. After revoking, the app cannot reach your computer until you approve it again on that computer.
  • Privacy settings: tell Olympus in your own words what is private for you, and mark folders, labels or senders as always Private, in the Olympus panel.
  • Anonymous questions: off unless you set them up; turn them off at any time in the Olympus dashboard on your computer, on its Private answers page.
  • Revoke account access: remove Olympus's access in your Google or Dropbox account settings.
  • Stop Olympus: olympus engine uninstall stops Olympus and removes its login item (on Omarchy, its user service). It keeps your settings and data.
  • Export your data: olympus data export --output <folder> exports supported indexed data and sanitized settings. Credentials and some operational state are left out; the export's manifest lists what was included and skipped.
  • Delete your data: olympus data delete --all deletes the index and data Olympus stored on your computer (add --dry-run first to see what it will remove). This cannot be undone.
  • Relay registry: ask us to remove your install's registry entry by emailing support.

Connected sources can contain sensitive information, such as health or financial records, government identifiers, or passwords. Security

Connections are encrypted in transit. An AI app must be approved on your computer before it can reach it, and through the relay only the published clients of ChatGPT, Claude, Grok and Grok Bot are accepted. The relay cannot issue tokens or approve new connections. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach affecting you, we will tell you as the law requires.

Olympus is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly process personal data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has used Olympus, contact us and we will delete any data we hold about them.
